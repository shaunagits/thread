Same dashboard. Three ways to make it a drawing instead of a screenshot.

All three keep the operations panel, its content and its six callouts. Nothing here invents a colour, a font or a spacing value. What changes is how the annotation behaves, and in one case whether the plate needs side margins at all.

Every direction animates on load, CSS only, no JavaScript, roughly 1.6 to 1.9 seconds, and sits behind prefers-reduced-motion so the default for anyone who asks for less motion is the finished still drawing. Reload the page to replay.

A. The plate

The panel stops pretending to be a screenshot. No white card, no rounded corners, no boxes around the figures: one hairline weight throughout, drawn straight onto the paper. A double plate frame surrounds the whole composition and two vertical rules separate the annotation margins from the field, so the numerals sit in a margin the way they would on a botanical plate rather than floating next to the drawing. Plate number bottom right.

What it buys. The annotation becomes the subject, because the panel is no longer competing for attention. What it costs. It is the most conservative of the three and it is still 1180 units wide, so the margins still pan sideways on a phone.

Motion. The plate frame draws itself first, then the six leader lines in sequence with the badges landing behind them. It reads like someone annotating a drawing while you watch, which is exactly the thing Thread sells.

B. The cutaway

The panel geometry is untouched, coordinate for coordinate. One thing changes: the leader lines stop dying at the panel edge. Each one crosses into the drawing as a dotted segment and terminates on the specific element it is talking about, and that element is marked. Callout 1 lands on the open-jobs figure. Callout 2 lands on the chart. Callout 3 runs the full width of a job row to the AUTO tag, and the row is washed to show which one. Callout 6 runs back along the status bar.

Why this is the real fix. Right now six annotations point at a rectangle. A reader has to guess which part of the panel each sentence is about, and mostly does not bother. Once the leaders land on things, the drawing explains itself in the order you drew it. This is the difference between a caption and an annotation.

What it costs. Least visual change of the three, so if you wanted the hero to feel new, this will not read as new. Same 1180 width, same sideways pan on a phone.

Motion. The solid margin segment draws, then the dotted segment travels inward, then the target marks itself. The eye is walked from the sentence to the thing.

C. Legend below

The side margins are deleted. Numbered markers sit directly on the drawing at the six points they refer to, and the sentences move to a numbered legend under a rule. That is standard field-guide practice, and it means the plate no longer has to be 1180 units wide to hold two columns of prose: it is 900, and the legend is three columns that become one on a phone.

Why I would pick this one. The margins are what force the sideways pan on mobile, and the sideways pan is where the entire selling argument currently disappears. Directions A and B both need a separate phone composition to fix that. C is the phone composition. It also puts the markers on the things they describe, so it inherits B's craft gain for free.

What it costs. The reading order becomes two steps, marker then legend, rather than one glance. On a wide screen a margin annotation is slightly more elegant. Worth trading.

Motion. Markers land on the drawing one at a time and each legend entry fades in with its marker, so the numbering teaches itself.

The phone composition

Shown at 390 units, which is an iPhone in portrait. This is a second SVG that swaps in under the 820px breakpoint with a plain display:none, so it costs no JavaScript and no container queries. Panel simplified to two columns of figures, table kept, TODAY list dropped because it is the least load-bearing block. Legend stacks to one column. Nothing pans sideways and the Scroll sideways to read hint disappears.

The finding worth acting on: this is the same answer for all three directions. A and B need it bolted on. C already is it, which is most of why C is my recommendation.

What I would do

C, with B's targeting, and A's plate frame as an option. They are not really mutually exclusive. C is the layout decision, B is the annotation decision, and A is a surface treatment you can apply to either. If you want one answer: ship C.
