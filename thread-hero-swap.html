Six ways to run the swap.

Same wireframe panel, same five connected boxes, same icons trading places. Four are about how the boxes connect to the panel. Two are about how the trade is timed.

Set one is email, reporting, invoicing, shipping and payments. Set two is scheduling, crews, site, permits and checklists. Set three, where it appears, is bookings, messages, orders, reviews and mobile.

1. Fan

Curves converge to one node

What you already saw. The node is the moment of collection, and the panel is what comes out of it. Softest of the four.

2. Rail

Short drops onto one bus, bus into the node

Schematic rather than organic. Reads like a wiring diagram, which is the closest of the four to the field guide language the rest of the site is drawn in.

3. Ports

Each one plugs straight into the panel, no node

The panel does the collecting, so the node disappears. Quietest of the four and the most literal about plugging in. Loses the single point of convergence, which you may or may not want.

4. Dock

Boxes sit on the panel edge, no threads at all

Half the height of the others, which for something sitting above the fold is a real advantage. The boxes become the panel's own header, so the swap reads as the panel reconfiguring itself rather than things feeding into it.

5. Rolling

Timing variant · one box changes at a time

Same Fan composition. Each box runs the same swap on its own offset, so something is always changing but never everything at once, and the panel shows mixed sets most of the time. That mixture is arguably the truest picture: nobody's five tools come from one catalogue.

6. Three sets

Timing variant · rotates through three trades, not two

A two set toggle can read as "we do these two kinds of business." A third breaks that and makes it read as a sample of something open ended. Eighteen second cycle, six seconds a set.

Mixing

The four compositions and the two timings are independent. Pick one of each and I will build it: Fan, Rail, Ports or Dock, then together, rolling, or three sets. Rolling and three sets can also be combined, though at that point almost nothing is ever still.

Note on set three: its first slot reuses the calendar, because bookings and scheduling are the same drawing. If the repeat bothers you across the rotation, say so and I will draw a distinct one.
