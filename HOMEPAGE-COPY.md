*Note for later: §02's heading, "One line through everything you run," now makes

**Lead** *(revised 14 Aug 2026, 45 words to 15)*

*Chosen by the owner after rejecting roughly a dozen alternatives. What the
rejections were consistently about, and what any future rewrite has to respect:

sentences, no vocabulary they wouldn't use themselves.*

another tool", "separate jobs", "closes the gap", "handoffs", "a person in the
middle", "outgrown the tools they run on".*

*"Not the other way around" is a separate sentence deliberately. As a trailing
comma clause it dribbles; as its own sentence the contrast lands.*

## § 01 — The problem

**Heading**

> Nothing is broken. That's the problem.

**Body**

> Every tool you bought works. The accounting software does accounting. The
> payment processor takes payments. The scheduling app schedules. Each one is
> fine on its own, and none of them know the others exist.
>
> So a person becomes the connection between them. Someone reads a number off
> one screen and types it into another. Someone opens four tabs to answer one
> question. Someone remembers to send the invoice. That work never appears as a
> line item on anything, which is exactly why it never gets fixed.
>
> That work is what Thread removes.

**Margin note**, *replaces the current one, which cites system counts from
clients you don't have yet*

> **Worth asking before anything else**
>
> What does a customer touch, from first contact to final payment? Which of
> those steps happens because a person remembers to do it? Most businesses
> can't answer the first question from a single screen. That gap is the work.

*Label stays "Typical intake" or becomes "Worth asking". Either fits the layout.*

## § 02 — How it works

**Heading**

> One line through everything you run.

**Lead**

> Thread doesn't replace the tools you already pay for. It builds the layer that
> runs through them, so information moves on its own and your team looks in one
> place.

**Figure caption**, unchanged: *Fig. 2 — The thread, drawn*

**Steps**

*The brand guide proposes four (Understand, Plan, Build, Launch). The built
component takes three, and planning is genuinely part of the map. Keeping three.*

**01 — MAP**
**Thread draws your systems**
> One conversation, then about a week. Thread traces how a single workflow
> actually moves through your business and hands back a labelled map of every
> tool, handoff and gap, with a ranked list of what's worth fixing first. Free,
> and yours to keep whether or not you go further.

**02 — BUILD**
**Thread closes the gaps**
> Custom apps, dashboards and automation built against your real process. You
> see working software every two weeks, not a status update.

**03 — HAND OVER**
**Thread documents and steps back**
> Every build ships with a written plate explaining what it does and why. It
> runs in your accounts, on your data. Ongoing support is available and it is
> optional.

**Care plan**, *from `[CONFIRM: $X]`/month, offered in every proposal*

*Neither your brand guide nor the site treats support as a service. It should be
one. It is the margin, it is the stability, and it is how a project becomes a
relationship. Put it in the proposal as a line item, not in a conversation after
launch, or the attach rate is near zero.*

**Rescue work**, *a short callout, not a fifth service tile*
> Already have something half-built? Thread also takes on software somebody else
> started: a tool that mostly works, an automation nobody understands, a system
> the person who built it no longer supports.

*Why this earns its space: owners and contractors can now generate a
working-ish tool quickly, and a year later nobody owns it. That population is
growing, and those businesses have a problem they already know about. Higher urgency and a shorter sales cycle than net

payment behaviour, higher failure rate, and it contradicts the operations
positioning that's doing the work. Keep one line on the contact form so you
don't turn away a good one:

> Occasionally I take on MVP work for founders. Ask.

## § 04 — The systems map *(replaces the invented Products section)*

*This section removes three invented products with invented pricing from a live,
indexable page and replaces them with something real that you control entirely.*

**Suggested name: Systems Map.** Plain, descriptive, searchable, and it already
matches the CTA on the site. Alternatives if you want something more ownable:
*Thread Map*, *Business Systems Map*, *Fig. 1*.

**Heading**

> Start with a map, not a quote.

**Lead**

> Before anyone talks about building anything, Thread draws how your work
> actually moves today. One workflow, start to finish. Every tool it touches,
> every handoff, every point where a person is doing a job a system should be
> doing. You get the map, a list of where time is going, and a ranked view of
> what's worth fixing first.
>
> It's free, it's yours to keep, and there's no obligation to do anything with
> it. If the honest answer is that you don't need custom software, the map will
> say so.

**What happens**

> **You fill in a short intake.** Ten minutes. What the business does, what the
> process is, which tools it touches, what already went wrong.
>
> **One conversation, ninety minutes.** Thread walks the process with whoever
> actually does it, not just whoever owns it.
>
> **You get the map within five business days.** A drawn current-state diagram,
> an inventory of where time and information leak, and a ranked list of fixes
> with rough cost and effort against each one.

**Margin note**, *this is where the limit lives*

> **Two per month**
>
> A map is real work, and it's free, so it's capped at two a month and one
> workflow each. Choose the process that costs you the most time: lead to
> invoice, job to payment, intake to delivery. Mapping a whole business is a
> paid engagement, and the free one is a reasonable way to find out whether
> it's worth doing.

**Button**

> **[ Start a systems map ]**

*One thing to add before this goes live: if you intend to publish maps
anonymised as proof (and you should), ask for that permission explicitly at the
end of the engagement, in writing. Do not bury it in the intake. Saying "no
obligation" and then publishing without a clear yes would undo exactly the trust
this section is built to create.*

*Full offer design, including the intake gate and the automation that keeps this
sustainable, is in `OFFER-AND-PIPELINE.md` §2. The short version: the intake form
does the data gathering and the qualifying, the deliverable is templated, and
each map costs you three to four hours rather than a week. Two a month is eight
hours. That is the constraint the offer is built around.*
